## Before the next professional demo

These six questions affect almost any editor in the room. Each closing test describes work still to do or repeat on the exact demo build.

### 1. “Can I trust the monitor when I make a one-frame decision?”

**Today.** Full, Half, Quarter and Auto change moving preview resolution on the newer build. Pausing restores full detail. Sixteen sampled animated-title frames passed the existing edge/overlap tests on a 1280×720 stage. At a scaled 966×544 stage, one of sixteen failed. The older minute-film’s 13 failures in 16 checks remain a separate result. A short Node project also passed six picture comparisons and bounded playback tests. Playing the finished video establishes that the delivery can play. Predicting it requires comparing the editable preview captured before rendering. [Preview controls](https://github.com/Tibule12/AutoPromote/blob/d3952ba6/reports/studio-visibility-preview-20261118/README.md) · [Text measurements](https://github.com/Tibule12/AutoPromote/blob/d3952ba6/reports/studio-completion-20261118/README.md) · [Node measurements](../reports/studio-node-repair-20261116/README.md)

**Still needed.** On the demo laptop, mark frames immediately before, on and after a cut with a caption, title, moving mask, B-roll and a dialogue cue. Compare those exact frames and the audible cue in the downloaded file. Repeat at the actual presentation window size. Record playback drops, seek delay, audio drift and which quality setting was needed. Scaled outlines, low-alpha edges and sustained loaded playback are the first things to inspect.

### 2. “Can I tighten this sentence without throwing everything else out of sync?”

**Today.** The timeline already offers split, ripple, roll, slip, slide, J/L audio cuts, snapping, locks and frame nudges. Groups can move child timing and keys together. A released 24 fps fractional-tail repair produced all 287 expected frames on its test cut. Editors can already try meaningful trim operations. Broader questions concern what moves with a cut when the same source appears twice, when a group crosses an edit or when speed varies. Group split/duplicate and attachment to a particular source occurrence are documented limits. **Still needed.** Use the same shot twice. Attach a title, caption and ducked music to the second use, shorten the first by a frame, roll the next cut and apply a speed ramp. The intended picture, words, mask and audio must move together, the locked item must keep its timing, and Undo/Redo, cold reopen and export must reproduce both decisions. Show an editor what is linked before they commit.

## An eight-minute demo an editor can drive

| Time | What the presenter does | What the editor gets to decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 min | Play a short, already-verified final with sound. Open its matching editable project. Keep the timeline and monitor visible together. | “Which cut, title or sound cue would you change first?” |
| 1–2 min | Open a completed Finder scan of owned footage. Play a suggestion and the surrounding source. | Choose the opening word and endpoint. Ask whether the ranking missed the actual payoff. |
| 2–4 min | Open the selection in Studio. Hand over the controls for a trim, a caption correction and a B-roll or title change. If using a prepared Director project, identify the project switch. | Watch whether they can find the layer, make the edit, Undo and Redo without coaching. |
| 4–6 min | Pause on the cut, inspect the full-detail frame, play through with sound and save. Close and reopen the project. | Have them find their corrected word, crop or moved key again. Ask what they expected to follow the cut. |
| 6–8 min | Show the export settings, estimate and actual delivery file. If a fresh render is pending, use a clearly identified previous finished version for playback. Compare the new version when available. | Ask for the file and handoff they would request on a paid job. Capture the exact spec and anything they would still need to finish elsewhere. |

Before the meeting, rehearse the exact build, account, screen size and audio output. Have an owned source, the matching project backup, a downloaded reference and the selected public tutorial to hand. For the revised edit, keep its input/project version, settings, completed receipt and actual movie together. A render queued during the meeting is a pending result.

## What to finish first

**Known product work** includes enforcing the export settings, supporting exact group/clip attachment and enabling the agreed handoff formats. **Qualification work** includes proving existing trims, mix controls, reframe and recovery on the pilot editor’s media. **Buyer-dependent requests** include HDR masters, a particular interchange format and client review roles. Ask which is a condition of accepting the job.

| Priority | Work to schedule | Evidence that closes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Next demo** | Start with preview/export agreement (S02), truthful export controls and reviewed captions. Use one dialogue cut with a title, B-roll, music and a difficult word. | Same project, same marked frames, specified file properties, audible cut, corrected captions, cold reopen and downloaded movie. Record failure at the actual monitor size as well as a full-raster result. |
| **First repeat editor** | Extend control coverage (S04) and per-occurrence/group ownership (S03). Add ordinary-account recovery, long/mixed-rate media and realistic multicam coverage. Address the pilot’s requested sidecar, stem or delivery format. | The editor brings unfamiliar footage and completes a client revision unassisted. Log time to first usable cut, correction time, drift, dropouts, failed jobs and recovery steps. Tie each result to its build and source. |
| **Agency or finishing pilot** | Prioritise the receiving editor’s interchange format, client-scoped review, versioned brand variants and tested batch delivery. Scope specialist HDR, advanced 3D or generated-media work against an actual brief. | A second editor opens the project or translated timeline on another machine. A reviewer identifies a frame, approves the revision, and the recipient accepts the delivered files against the brief. |

The project’s full qualification requires normal entry, timeline visibility, meaningful manual changes, preview and interactive adjustment, combination with other tools, Undo/Redo, save, cold reopen, exact recovery, actual export and a pre-final comparison against that export. Track a pass against the specific project, account and build. At each demo, record the editor’s role, source type, requested deliverables and the first action where they needed help or opened another tool. That is the most direct way to turn these priorities into the next product decision.
