## Implemented waste reduction and remaining hot paths

`backgroundActivityService` now excludes account, allowance and connection polling from cross-host demand. A 24-hour/15-second polling regression verifies that no further activity writes occur and optional demand expires. Existing event-driven queue watchers still wake due schedules/retries while the UI is closed; no scheduled publication is lost. The complete worker/dispatch regression remains passing.

The existing queue cache avoids a new Firestore query on every idle timer tick. Demand-scoped analytics, bounded platform observation windows, immutable upload hashes, stream-based transfers and the cheap `/api/ping` health check are retained. Do not point uptime monitors at verbose health/integration scans. New media changes remove a redundant full-size ingest encode, repeat transcription, duplicate VFX dispatches and an extra neutral finish encode.

The observed query volume is too large to attribute to seven November MAUs without further endpoint/query telemetry. For scale, a 50-document query every five seconds for 30 days causes 25.92 million reads, even if nobody creates new content. That illustrates polling amplification; it does not prove a particular endpoint caused the observed reads. Leader renewal every 45 seconds also creates persistent reads/writes per host, while cleanup and diagnostic cron continue when users are inactive. Keep necessary maintenance, coalesce it and instrument its real work before changing frequency. The account-poll fix does not claim to have eliminated the observed 32 million reads in production.

Frontend live logs have three bounded listeners; admin log polling runs every two seconds, system health every ten seconds and platform health every minute. Not every local animation interval performs network I/O. Visibility-aware cancellation/coalescing and removal of unused subscriptions are further candidates; this continuation does not silently change their refresh/visibility semantics or claim measured savings from them.

Render bandwidth is shared across the workspace. A reference 100 GB month on Hobby is approximately `(100 − 5) × $0.15 = $14.25` bandwidth above compute. At 1,000 GB it is $149.25. Moving from Hobby's 5 GB to Pro's 25 GB only saves at most $3 in bandwidth while adding $25 workspace cost; this alone does not justify upgrading. Keep efficient private media delivery and control-plane payloads small. Cloud-side publishing near GCS may remove one Render media leg, but needs its own bounded adapter and provider-upload qualification.

No measured production CPU/RSS/latency/load curve was available through Render. A proposed engineering trigger is sustained memory pressure near the instance limit/OOM or CPU/queue/p95 latency saturation under normal API traffic after media work is separated. Registered-user counts alone do not determine replicas. The million-user financial rows do not certify that one $7 server can serve a million users; scaling overhead must be added from load tests.
