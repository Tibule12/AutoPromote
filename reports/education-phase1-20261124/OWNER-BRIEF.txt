# AUTOPROMOTE EDUCATION — CREATIVE TECHNOLOGY LEARNING PLATFORM

## 1. New strategic direction

AutoPromote is expanding beyond its commercial creative technology platform into educational digital skills development.

The objective is to provide high school learners, particularly learners in underserved South African communities, with free, limited and supervised access to modern creative technology.

The educational initiative must teach practical, transferable skills in video editing, visual effects, motion graphics, compositing, digital storytelling and responsible artificial intelligence use.

Our initial focus is **Viral Clip Studio**, the main creative editing environment within AutoPromote.

This is not simply a documentation page, FAQ section, collection of promotional videos or superficial product tour. We are building a structured, practical creative technology learning platform.

## 2. Inspect the existing repository first

Before implementing anything, thoroughly inspect the existing AutoPromote codebase.

Identify:

- All implemented Viral Clip Studio features.
- All available editing tools and controls.
- Effects and their adjustable parameters.
- Timeline operations.
- Layer management.
- Transform controls.
- Keyframe functionality.
- Masks and compositing.
- Motion and Flow functionality.
- AI-assisted editing capabilities.
- Background removal and replacement.
- Preview and rendering behaviour.
- Export capabilities.
- Existing tutorial infrastructure.
- Existing sample media and test assets.

Determine which features are genuinely functional, partially implemented, broken or unavailable.

Do not invent tools, controls, parameters or capabilities that do not exist.

Do not classify functionality as educationally ready merely because automated tests pass.

## 3. Build a dedicated AutoPromote Education section

Create a dedicated Education section that integrates with the existing AutoPromote website and design system.

Include:

- Education landing page.
- Explanation of the initiative.
- Benefits for learners.
- Benefits for teachers and schools.
- Creative technology career pathways.
- Structured learning library.
- Beginner, intermediate and advanced learning paths.
- Individual lesson pages.
- Practical assignments.
- Learning progress tracking.
- School partnership information.
- Sponsor and funding partnership information.

The Education section must feel like a genuine modern learning platform rather than a generic marketing template.

## 4. Studio-first curriculum

Create a structured curriculum based on verified Viral Clip Studio functionality.

Organize lessons progressively around the following areas where supported by the actual implementation:

### Foundation

- Understanding digital video.
- Introduction to creative storytelling.
- Understanding the Studio interface.
- Importing and organizing media.
- Understanding the timeline.
- Video trimming and splitting.
- Layers and track organization.
- Audio management.
- Text and captions.
- Exporting completed projects.

### Intermediate editing

- Video transitions.
- Transformations and positioning.
- Scaling and rotation.
- Keyframes and animation.
- Colour correction and grading.
- Speed manipulation.
- Audio synchronisation.
- Masks and compositing.
- Background removal and replacement.
- Motion graphics.

### Advanced creative techniques

- Visual effects.
- Subject tracking.
- Advanced masking.
- Multi-layer compositing.
- Motion and Flow compositions.
- Animated typography.
- Cinematic effects.
- Advanced keyframe animation.
- 3D workflows where genuinely supported.
- AI-assisted creative production.

Only publish lessons for functionality that has been verified.

Features that are incomplete or unreliable must remain unpublished until they work correctly.

## 5. Mandatory lesson structure

Every individual lesson must contain:

1. Lesson title.
2. Difficulty level.
3. Estimated completion time.
4. Learning objectives.
5. Explanation of the creative technique.
6. Real-world applications of the technique.
7. Required practice assets.
8. Original source footage.
9. Actual completed target-result video.
10. Detailed step-by-step instructions.
11. Exact Studio controls used.
12. Exact parameter values where applicable.
13. Explanation of what changing each parameter does.
14. Screenshots showing the actual Studio interface.
15. Narrated tutorial demonstrating the complete workflow.
16. Common mistakes and troubleshooting.
17. Practical student assignment.
18. Assessment criteria.
19. Lesson completion tracking.
20. Next recommended lesson.

Tutorials must teach students both how to operate the software and why the underlying creative technique matters.

## 6. Proof-driven tutorial production

This requirement is non-negotiable.

Every demonstrated creative result must be produced using the actual AutoPromote Studio implementation.

For each tutorial:

- Use real moving source footage.
- Execute the demonstrated editing workflow.
- Record the actual Studio interface.
- Apply the actual controls and parameter values.
- Show the moving preview.
- Export the completed result.
- Inspect the rendered output.
- Confirm that the preview and export agree.
- Include the original footage and final result in the lesson.

Do not use fabricated interface screenshots.

Do not use externally generated finished videos while claiming they were produced by AutoPromote.

Do not substitute static images for moving video demonstrations.

Do not claim completion based only on screenshots, test results or successful HTTP responses.

Where effects involve tracking, masking, compositing or background removal, inspect the output across the entire relevant video duration for visual defects, flickering, disappearing subjects and incorrect motion.

If a feature cannot reliably reproduce the demonstrated result, report the limitation and do not publish its tutorial.

## 7. Guided practical learning

Where technically appropriate, provide an interactive learning experience.

Students should be able to:

- Watch a lesson.
- Preview the expected result.
- Open the relevant Studio workspace.
- Load a prepared practice project.
- Access the supplied source media.
- Follow guided instructions.
- Adjust the actual Studio controls.
- Preview their work.
- Complete and export their own project.

Explore deep linking into the Studio and reusable lesson project templates.

Do not build a fake Studio simulator that is disconnected from the real editing engine.

## 8. Tutorial media production system

Establish a reusable tutorial production pipeline.

Where appropriate, use existing browser automation, Playwright, FFmpeg and available media tooling to produce:

- Actual interface recordings.
- Narrated demonstrations.
- Highlighted controls.
- Cursor guidance.
- Readable annotations.
- Before-and-after comparisons.
- Exported proof videos.
- Captions and transcripts.
- Downloadable practice assets.

Narration must explain what is being done, why it is being done and what result the student should expect.

Do not merely generate a video that moves a cursor around the interface without teaching anything.

Use legally reusable or original practice footage. Maintain records of asset permissions and attribution where necessary.

Design the learning content so that English and isiXhosa versions can be supported later.

## 9. Educational relevance

Lessons must connect creative techniques to practical student opportunities, including:

- School presentations.
- Digital storytelling.
- School documentaries.
- Interviews.
- Creative arts projects.
- Entrepreneurship.
- Business Studies promotional assignments.
- School media clubs.
- Film and television production.
- Digital marketing.
- Creative portfolios.

Explain how the skills transfer beyond AutoPromote to other professional creative tools and workflows.

Do not claim that the programme is CAPS-approved, accredited or endorsed by education authorities unless formal approval has actually been obtained.

## 10. Education account architecture

Plan a separate educational entitlement system supporting:

- School-managed accounts.
- Teacher and learner roles.
- Restricted processing credits.
- Storage limits.
- Rendering duration limits.
- Usage monitoring.
- Cost controls.
- Private student projects.
- Teacher supervision.
- Disabled public publishing by default.
- No compulsory purchases.
- No targeted advertising to learners.
- Appropriate privacy, consent and safeguarding controls.
- School-level participation and learning outcome reports.

Do not automatically grant unlimited free processing to educational users.

Do not onboard minors or enable school registration in production until the necessary safeguards and permissions are established.

## 11. Implementation priorities

Phase 1 must focus on:

1. Complete audit of existing Studio functionality.
2. Education landing page.
3. Structured Studio learning library.
4. Reusable lesson architecture.
5. Actual tutorial production pipeline.
6. First five complete, proof-verified beginner lessons.
7. Practice projects and downloadable source assets.
8. End-to-end testing of the learning experience.

After the first five lessons are genuinely complete, continue expanding the curriculum.

Do not prioritize Cam Combiner, Publisher, Scheduler or unrelated platform development during this initial educational implementation.

Do not deploy to production or initiate paid cloud/GPU workloads without my approval.

## 12. Acceptance criteria

The first five lessons are complete only when a beginner can follow the published instructions, use the actual Studio controls and reproduce the demonstrated creative result.

Provide actual working lesson pages, tutorial videos, source assets, exported proof videos and tested practice projects.

A written implementation report, passing tests, mockups or placeholder tutorials alone will not constitute completion.

The long-term objective is to develop AutoPromote Education into a credible digital creative skills initiative that schools, teachers, learners, corporate sponsors and funding institutions can participate in.

Begin by inspecting the existing repository, establish the implementation architecture and proceed with the first phase.
